Privacy policy, GSS Budget Receipts

Last updated: 2026-11-26

What this is

GSS Budget Receipts is part of the Budget section of a private household budgeting tool. It reads receipt emails from a connected Gmail account and matches them to bank transactions, so each purchase can be given a category. Only people on an allowlist can sign in.

What Google data it accesses

It asks for these from your Google account:

  • Read-only access to your Gmail (the gmail.readonly scope).
  • Your email address.
  • Your Google account id (the openid scope), which identifies the account.

It searches your mail for messages that look like receipts: messages from a list of sender addresses, or with subject words such as "receipt", "order confirmation", "invoice" or "refund". The search skips spam, trash and drafts. It then reads the text of the messages it finds. It never sends, deletes, labels or changes any mail.

What it keeps

It keeps only the fields it pulls out of each receipt:

  • The merchant, the dates, the currency, the total, tax and shipping.
  • The type of payment and the last four digits of the card, if the email shows them.
  • The order number and the line items (description, amount and quantity).
  • The sender's domain and Gmail's own message id for the email.
  • Which bank transaction the receipt matches, and the category suggested for it.

It also keeps your Google refresh token, encrypted, and some details about the connection: the Gmail address you linked, your Google account id, the Budget login the connection belongs to, its status, the scope you granted, when you agreed, and when it last ran. The key that decrypts the token is not stored in the database.

It does not keep the email body, the subject line or any attachment.

How the emails are processed

The text of each matching email is sent to Anthropic's API, which pulls out the fields listed above. We send it for this purpose only. We then keep the fields and discard the text.

Who can see it

The people signed in to this household's budget can see the receipt fields on a transaction, including receipts from your mailbox. Only you get the link that opens the original email in Gmail. We do not sell your data, share it with anyone else, or use it for advertising.

Where it is stored

The data is stored and processed on Cloudflare: a D1 database and Workers.

Disconnecting and deleting your data

In the app, open Budget, then Receipts, and choose Disconnect. You can also tick the option to delete your extracted receipts. When you disconnect:

  • The app stops reading your mailbox.
  • It asks Google to revoke its access, and deletes the stored refresh token.
  • If you chose to delete your receipts, the extracted receipts, their items and matches are removed. If you did not, they stay. Categories already applied to transactions stay either way.
  • The connection record stays, marked as revoked. It holds no token and no Gmail address, but it still holds your Google account id, the Budget login, the scope you granted, when you agreed, and when it last ran.

The app has no way to delete that record yet. To have your stored data deleted, including that record, contact the site owner through goodstuffsoftware.com.

You can also remove access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Google API Services User Data Policy

GSS Budget Receipts' use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Bank links (Plaid)

What it is. The people allowed into Budget can link a bank login through Plaid, so transactions arrive without statement files. Your bank username and password go to Plaid, never to this site. Plaid is told who is linking by a short code scrambled from the Budget login, not by the login itself.

What it reads. Through Plaid, it reads:

  • Transactions for up to two years: the date, amount, description, merchant name, and whether the bank shows it as pending or posted.
  • Each account's name, type, last four digits, balances and credit limit.
  • For credit cards, mortgages and student loans, whichever of these the bank provides: the interest rate, minimum payment, next monthly payment, due date, whether the account is overdue, the past-due amount, last statement date and balance, last payment amount and date, escrow balance, original loan amount and date, interest and principal paid so far this year, and payoff or maturity dates.

It does not ask Plaid for routing numbers or identity details. Plaid's card and loan data can arrive with full account numbers and property or lender addresses; the app's server ignores them and never uses, stores or logs them.

What it keeps, and how. It keeps those fields in a private database on Cloudflare. In transaction descriptions, account names and the bank's name, any run of five or more digits, and any number of nine or more digits written in groups (for example with spaces or dashes), is cut to its last four digits; shorter numbers such as store numbers and dates stay as written, and so does a phone number written with spaces or dashes in groups (for example 555-123-4567); a phone number typed as one unbroken run of ten digits is cut like any other long number. It also keeps the bank's name, the Budget login that linked it, when that link was made (the time of the agreement shown with it), the link's status, and Plaid's own reference numbers for the link, its accounts and its transactions. The access key Plaid issues is stored encrypted (AES-256-GCM), and the key that decrypts it is held only by the server's secret store, not in the database. Sync logs hold counts and codes, never content.

Agreeing. Each person is shown what is read and kept, and asked to agree, before their own first bank link; the time that link was made is stored with it as the time of agreement. Each link carries its own time; it is not the first time the person agreed.

How it is used. Only to show the household's own budget. We do not sell it, share it with anyone else, use it for advertising, or send it to any AI service.

Who can see it. The people signed in to this household's budget can see every linked account and transaction. Only the person who linked a bank can unlink it, reconnect it, or choose which Budget account each of its accounts feeds. Plaid also handles the data under its own privacy policy: Plaid's end user privacy policy.

Unlinking and deleting. In the app, open Budget, then Banks, and choose Unlink on the bank. When you unlink:

  • The app asks Plaid to end its access, and deletes the stored access key, the sync position, and the card and loan details Plaid supplied (such as the due dates and payment amounts).
  • Transactions already added to Budget stay as household records. So does the balance and interest-rate history the sync added to each debt, as part of that debt's history. So do the bank's name, each account's masked name, type, last four digits, balances, available balances and credit limits, Plaid's reference numbers for the link, its accounts and its transactions, the record of who linked it and when that link was made, and the counts and codes from past syncs.
  • Unlinking does not give the link back: Plaid's free plan allows a fixed number of bank links for life, and the app keeps a count that stays for good, holding Plaid's reference number for each link, the bank's Plaid code and the Budget login that started it.

The app has no way to delete those remaining records yet. To have them deleted, contact the site owner (below). If you revoke access at your bank or through Plaid, the app stops syncing and deletes the same items once Plaid reports it.
